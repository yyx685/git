# Submission Extras for Autonomic Neuroscience: Basic and Clinical

## Highlights

- SCG is a major sympathetic relay for the proximal cerebral circulation.
- SCG tone is modest at rest but becomes pathogenic in stroke and SAH.
- SCG remodelling involves ion channels, purinergic signalling, and immune cells.
- SCG block and electroacupuncture are emerging cerebrovascular neuromodulation strategies.

## Graphical Abstract Description

A two-panel schematic. The left panel shows the normal SCG–cerebrovascular axis: preganglionic sympathetic input from the upper thoracic spinal cord synapses in the SCG, and postganglionic fibres travel along the internal carotid plexus to the proximal cerebral arteries, with lower tonic vasoconstriction under physiological conditions. The right panel shows pathological conditions: hypertension, cerebral ischemia, and subarachnoid hemorrhage drive SCG hyperexcitability through ion-channel and purinergic remodelling, causing sympathetic overactivation, cerebral vasoconstriction, hypoperfusion, vasospasm, neurogenic inflammation, and vascular remodelling. An arrow indicates therapeutic interruption by SCG block, endovascular transmural block, or electroacupuncture.

## Figure Legends

### Figure 1
Anatomical organization of the superior cervical ganglion–cerebrovascular axis. Preganglionic sympathetic fibres from the upper thoracic spinal cord synapse in the SCG. Postganglionic fibres ascend with the internal carotid nerve and distribute to the internal carotid artery, the circle of Willis, and proximal pial arteries. Additional projections to the pineal gland, eye, and upper cervical nerves are indicated.

### Figure 2
Pathological SCG remodelling and therapeutic targeting in cerebrovascular disease. Hypertension, cerebral ischemia, and subarachnoid hemorrhage promote SCG hyperexcitability through altered ion channels, purinergic signalling, and neuroimmune interactions. Excessive SCG output contributes to cerebral vasoconstriction, hypoperfusion, vasospasm, neurogenic inflammation, and vascular remodelling. SCG blockade, endovascular transmural blockade, and electroacupuncture may interrupt this sympathetic-mediated injury loop.
