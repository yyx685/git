# Section 3 — English Draft

**Section title:**  
Electrophysiological and molecular properties of superior cervical ganglion neurons

---

## 3.1 Ion channel repertoire and intrinsic excitability

The firing behaviour and synaptic output of superior cervical ganglion (SCG) neurons are governed by a rich repertoire of voltage- and ligand-gated ion channels. Classical studies established that nicotinic acetylcholine receptors mediate fast excitatory transmission, while voltage-gated sodium, calcium, and potassium channels shape action potential generation, repetitive firing, and transmitter release (Alkadhi et al., 2005). More recent work has highlighted the importance of intracellular signalling lipids in tuning these channels. For example, phosphoinositide-dependent regulation of M-type potassium channels and other conductances dynamically adjusts sympathetic neuron excitability in response to receptor activation (Kruse et al., 2016).

The electrophysiological phenotype of SCG neurons is not fixed but is remodelled by disease. In a rabbit model of myocardial infarction, SCG neurons showed increased action potential firing and altered activation and inactivation properties of sodium currents, together with changes in potassium currents, indicating that cardiac injury induces intrinsic sympathetic plasticity (Cheng et al., 2023). Similar channel remodelling has been observed after myocardial ischemia, and treatment with fluvastatin attenuated some of these abnormalities, suggesting that lipid-lowering or pleiotropic drug effects may normalise SCG excitability (Cheng et al., 2018). These findings imply that SCG hyperexcitability may be both a consequence of, and a contributor to, sustained sympathetic overdrive in cardiovascular disease.

## 3.2 Purinergic signalling in injury and disease

Purinergic receptors are among the most intensively studied molecular sensors in SCG neurons because they link tissue injury, inflammation, and sympathoexcitation. P2X and P2Y receptors are upregulated or functionally altered in several pathological models. In myocardial ischemia, P2X7 receptor expression and current amplitude increase in rat SCG neurons, and pharmacological inhibition of P2X7 reduces the associated sympathoexcitatory response (Zhang et al., 2023). A parallel line of work in diabetic cardiac autonomic neuropathy has implicated P2Y14 and P2Y12 receptors, with natural compounds such as naringin exerting protective effects through these pathways (Tang et al., 2022). P2Y12 signalling in SCG neurons has also been linked to abnormal sympathoexcitatory reflexes following myocardial ischemia (Zou et al., 2022).

Recent studies extend purinergic mechanisms to neuroinflammation and neuropathic states. The P2Y13 receptor contributes to HIV-1 gp120-induced SCG neuropathy through activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, indicating that purinergic signalling can convert immune or toxic signals into neuronal injury and aberrant firing (Yin et al., 2024). Resident macrophages within cervical sympathetic ganglia participate in P2Y12-mediated diabetic neuropathy, highlighting that purinergic signalling is not confined to neurons but operates across neuron–immune interactions (Du et al., 2025). Collectively, these data position purinergic receptors as promising targets for interrupting pathological SCG sensitisation.

## 3.3 Synaptic plasticity and neurotrophin dependence

SCG neurons exhibit long-term synaptic potentiation (LTP), a form of activity-dependent plasticity first demonstrated at ganglionic nicotinic synapses more than four decades ago (Brown & McAfee, 1982). Unlike hippocampal LTP, SCG LTP does not require NMDA receptors and is instead modulated by neurotrophic factors and other local signals (Alkadhi et al., 2005). Brain-derived neurotrophic factor and nerve growth factor differentially regulate LTP in the SCG, and functional TrkB receptors are expressed by SCG neurons (Arias et al., 2014; Valle-Leija et al., 2017). This neurotrophin-dependent plasticity provides a mechanism by which target-derived or activity-dependent signals can persistently adjust ganglionic gain.

The strength of SCG LTP is not uniform across the ganglion or across physiological states. LTP is differentially expressed in rostral and caudal SCG neurons, and this rostrocaudal difference is altered in hypertensive rats, suggesting that hypertension reorganises synaptic function in a region-specific manner (Martínez et al., 2020). More recently, KCNQ potassium channel function has been shown to influence LTP and its neurotrophin-dependent modulation in the rat SCG, linking intrinsic channel properties to synaptic plasticity (Arias et al., 2023). These observations support a model in which ion channels, neurotrophins, and activity-dependent plasticity together determine the integrated output of the SCG.

## 3.4 Transcriptomic heterogeneity and molecular identity

A major recent advance is the recognition that SCG neurons are not a homogeneous population. Single-cell and spatial transcriptomic studies have defined distinct SCG neuronal subtypes, each with characteristic expression of ion channels, neuropeptides, and receptors, and have linked these molecular profiles to innervation of specific cranial targets (Senturk et al., 2026). This target-organ-specific organisation implies that different SCG subsets may be selectively recruited in different diseases, rather than the ganglion responding as a single unit.

Bulk transcriptomic analysis in a rat model of hypertension-induced left ventricular hypertrophy has revealed broad changes in SCG gene expression together with adrenal gland remodelling, suggesting that chronic pressure overload reprograms sympathetic molecular networks (Li et al., 2025). Integrating these transcriptomic data with electrophysiological and purinergic findings will be essential for identifying which SCG neuronal subtypes drive pathological sympathoexcitation and which are most suitable for targeted intervention.

## 3.5 Summary and knowledge gaps

SCG neurons possess a diverse ion channel and receptor repertoire, exhibit neurotrophin-dependent synaptic plasticity, and are molecularly heterogeneous. Disease states such as myocardial ischemia, diabetes, and hypertension remodel SCG excitability and purinergic signalling, thereby shifting the ganglion toward a hyperexcitable phenotype. Major gaps remain: the causal relationship between transcriptomic subtype identity and electrophysiological function is largely correlative; most disease-related channel and purinergic data derive from cardiac rather than cerebrovascular models; and the contribution of specific SCG subtypes to cerebral vascular control has not been defined. Addressing these gaps will be necessary before SCG molecular targets can be translated into cerebrovascular therapy.
